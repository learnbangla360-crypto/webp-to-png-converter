WebP was developed by Google to shrink images for the web. PNG is older and larger, but nearly every editor, printer driver and upload form understands it. Converting doesn't recover detail that a lossy WebP threw away. What you gain is compatibility and a format that won't degrade when you re-save it.

Expert tips and common mistakes

  • Don't expect a smaller file. WebP is built to be compact, so PNG is often bigger. If size matters, use Compress Image File afterward.
  • Choose JPG for photos you'll only share. If you don't need transparency, WebP to JPG Converter gives smaller files.
  • Check transparency before sending. A checkerboard thumbnail confirms it. A white box around a logo usually means the original wasn't transparent.
  • Crop first. Trim with Crop Image so you don't store pixels you'll discard.
  • Keep the original. A PNG made from a lossy WebP can't go back to the exact original bytes, so don't delete the source.

Troubleshooting

  • "Could not be read." The file may be damaged, or your browser can't decode that WebP. Try opening it in another browser, then export a fresh copy.
  • Only some files downloaded. Your browser blocked multiple downloads. Allow downloads for this site and convert again.
  • The PNG is huge. That's normal for photos. Pick 50% in Output size, or compress the PNG afterward.
  • Conversion fails on a big image. Your device ran out of canvas memory. Convert at 50% or 25%.
  • My animation became a still image. PNG output here is a single frame. Keep the WebP if you need the motion.
  • The background isn't transparent. The original WebP likely had a solid background. Use Background Remover to cut it out.
  • The file is a .webp but won't load. Some downloads are renamed images in other formats or are incomplete. Download the file again.

Glossary

  • WebP: an image format from Google that supports lossy and lossless compression, transparency and animation.
  • Lossy compression: shrinking a file by discarding detail.
  • Lossless compression: shrinking a file without discarding any pixel data, as PNG does.
  • Alpha channel: the layer that stores how transparent each pixel is.
  • Canvas: the browser feature that redraws your image so it can be saved in a new format.

Frequently asked questions

Is this WebP to PNG converter free?

Yes. You can convert as many images as you like at no cost. There's no watermark, no trial and no paid tier. Drop in your WebP files, press the button and the PNG files download straight to your device.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Your files are processed in your browser and are never uploaded. The page reads each WebP on your device, redraws it on a canvas and saves a PNG locally. Closing the tab clears everything from memory.

Do I need to sign up or install anything?

No. There's no account, no email and no app. Open the page in a modern browser such as Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, drop in your images and convert. It works on desktop, tablets and phones.

Does the PNG keep the transparent background?

Yes. If your WebP has transparent areas, they stay transparent in the PNG, because PNG supports an alpha channel. Opaque WebP images simply become opaque PNG files. Check the checkerboard behind each thumbnail to see transparency before you convert.

Does converting WebP to PNG improve quality?

Not on its own. A lossy WebP has already discarded some detail, and PNG can't restore it. A lossless WebP converts to PNG with identical pixels. Either way, the PNG won't lose anything more when you edit and re-save it.

What happens to an animated WebP?

PNG here is a still image, so an animated WebP becomes a single-frame PNG, normally the first frame. This tool doesn't build animated output. For motion, keep the original WebP or use a video-to-GIF tool.

Can I convert many WebP files at once?

Yes. You can add up to 100 images in one go. Each PNG downloads as its own file, one after another. Your browser may ask permission to download multiple files, so choose Allow when it does.

Why is the PNG bigger than my WebP?

WebP is designed to produce small files, often smaller than PNG or JPG at similar quality. PNG stores every pixel without loss, so photos in particular can grow a lot. If size matters, keep the WebP or compress the PNG afterward.

Is there a file size limit?

Each file can be up to 50 MB. Very large images may still fail on older phones because browsers limit canvas memory. If that happens, use the size option to convert at 50% or 25% of the original dimensions.

Does it work offline?

Partly. You need to load the page once. After that, the conversion itself needs no connection, so you can keep the tab open, go offline and still convert images that are already on your device.
